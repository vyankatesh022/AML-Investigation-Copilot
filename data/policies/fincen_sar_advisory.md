# FinCEN Advisory FIN-2023-A001: Guidance on Currency Structuring and Pass-Through Account Activity

**Issuing Agency:** Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), U.S. Department of the Treasury  
**Category:** Regulatory Compliance & Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR) Guidance  
**Target Audience:** Bank AML Compliance Officers, Transaction Monitoring Analysts  

---

## 1. Statutory Background: Currency Transaction Reporting and Anti-Structuring
Under 31 U.S.C. 5313 and 31 CFR 1010.311 (Bank Secrecy Act), financial institutions are legally required to file a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) for each deposit, withdrawal, exchange of currency, or other payment or transfer by, through, or to such financial institution which involves a transaction in currency of more than $10,000.

Structuring occurs when an individual or entity conducts currency transactions in amounts at or below $10,000 for the purpose of evading the CTR filing requirement. Under 31 U.S.C. 5324(a)(3), structuring is a federal criminal offense regardless of whether the underlying funds originate from legal or illegal activity.

---

## 2. Red Flag Indicators for Structuring (Smurfing)
Compliance monitoring systems and investigators must look for the following red flags:
* **Threshold Corridor Evasion:** Multiple transactions conducted just below the $10,000 threshold, specifically in the range of $9,000 to $9,999, within a 24- to 72-hour window.
* **Multi-Branch or Multi-Channel Activity:** An individual visiting different branches, using automated teller machines (ATMs), or initiating online transfers in rapid succession with amounts close to reporting limits.
* **Third-Party Structuring (Smurfing):** Multiple seemingly unrelated individuals depositing funds into a single beneficiary account in amounts slightly below regulatory thresholds.
* **Customer Inquiry or Resistance:** Customer explicitly inquiring about CTR thresholds, canceling or reducing a cash transfer after being informed of identification requirements.

---

## 3. Rapid Movement of Funds and Pass-Through Accounts (Mule Activity)
A primary hallmark of money laundering, cybercrime proceeds, and money mule networks is the rapid in-and-out movement of funds through newly opened or previously dormant accounts:
* **Account Balance Drain:** An incoming transfer or series of deposits followed immediately by an outgoing wire, transfer, or ATM cash withdrawal that depletes 90% or more of the account balance within 24 hours.
* **Lack of Ongoing Business Activity:** The account exhibits minimal day-to-day operational transactions (e.g., utility payments, payroll, groceries), functioning strictly as a pass-through transit point.
* **Discrepancy in Volume:** Transaction velocity and amounts are drastically disproportionate to the customer's declared occupation, age, or historical income baseline.

---

## 4. SAR Filing and Escalation Obligations
* When an analyst identifies clear patterns of structuring or rapid balance depletion with no reasonable commercial justification, a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) must be filed within 30 calendar days of initial detection.
* The SAR narrative must clearly detail:
  1. The chronological sequence of structured transactions and account balance deltas.
  2. Any observed customer behavior or counterparty links.
  3. The rationale for determining that the activity was designed to evade CTR reporting.
