# FATF Recommendation 16 & High-Risk Jurisdictions Guidance

**Issuing Body:** Financial Action Task Force (FATF)  
**Standard:** International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism & Proliferation  
**Subject:** Cross-Border Wire Transfers, High-Risk Jurisdictions, and Enhanced Due Diligence  

---

## 1. Scope of FATF Recommendation 16 (Wire Transfer Rule / Travel Rule)
Financial institutions must ensure that all cross-border wire transfers and domestic transfers are accompanied by complete, accurate, and verified originator and beneficiary information throughout the payment chain.

Mandatory Originator Information:
* Full legal name of the originator / sender.
* Originator account number or unique transaction reference number.
* Originator residential address, national identity number, or date and place of birth.

Mandatory Beneficiary Information:
* Full legal name of the recipient / beneficiary.
* Beneficiary account number or unique transaction identifier.

---

## 2. High-Risk and Sanctioned Jurisdictions
FATF continuously identifies jurisdictions with serious strategic deficiencies in their regimes to counter money laundering, terrorist financing, and proliferation financing.

* **Black List (Call for Action):** Jurisdictions subject to severe counter-measures and comprehensive sanctions (e.g., Democratic People's Republic of Korea - PRK, Iran - IRN, Myanmar - MMR).
* **Grey List (Increased Monitoring):** Jurisdictions actively working with the FATF to address strategic deficiencies (e.g., Syria - SYR, offshore secrecy jurisdictions like Cayman Islands - CYM, Panama - PAN).
* **Compliance Requirement:** Any wire transfer, remittance, or trade transaction routed to, from, or through a high-risk sanctioned jurisdiction must immediately trigger Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) and be escalated for executive compliance sign-off. If unauthorized, transactions must be blocked or rejected immediately under applicable sanctions regimes.

---

## 3. Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)
Under FATF Recommendation 12, financial institutions must implement risk management systems to determine whether a customer or beneficial owner is a foreign or domestic Politically Exposed Person (PEP), a family member, or a close associate.
* Transactions executed by PEPs carry inherent corruption and bribery risks.
* Any substantial transfer inconsistent with declared wealth, especially wires directed to offshore tax havens or private investment companies, must be subjected to stringent scrutiny.

---

## 4. Red Flags for Wire Transfers
Investigators should treat the following as critical red flags:
1. Wire transfers lacking originator address or identifying details.
2. Consecutive transfers executed just below international reporting limits.
3. Transactions involving counterparties in offshore financial secrecy jurisdictions without clear commercial economic rationale.
4. Fund transfers originating from personal accounts to corporate accounts in high-risk jurisdictions.
